# Free gate, fine-tuned: TinyBERT on labeled promise data

Measured 2026-11-03. Model: `huawei-noah/TinyBERT_General_4L_312D` (4-layer,
120 MB base weights; 55 MB fine-tuned checkpoint). The model card does not
state a license. Training: Apple M4, MPS, 25 seconds, no API calls and no model
spend.

## The question

The zero-shot runs in `21-model-comparison.md` closed one door: no free
checkpoint answered our gate questions well out of the box. Laya was closest at
11 of 12 manifest presence and 8 of 11 modality, but it kept 98 percent of 300
wild transcripts, so it saved almost no extraction calls. The remaining free
path was fine-tuning on labeled promise data.

## What the gate asks

The gate runs before extraction and answers one question:

- `needs_extraction`: did the agent commit to a financial action (binding,
  conditional, superseded, or retracted)?

Policy explanations, status updates, and requests for information are
`info_only` and should be skipped. The modality head still classifies all five
states so the result is comparable with the earlier model runs.

## Data and training

`bench/finetune/generate_data.py` builds 1,000 training rows, 250 validation
rows, and 250 held-out test rows from 50 scenario families. Labels are
constructed by the scenario, not generated by a model. Train and held-out rows
use different phrasings. The 12 committed eval cases are never used for
training, calibration, or threshold selection.

The recorded run fine-tunes TinyBERT 4-layer with a shared encoder and two
heads: the binary gate and a five-way modality head. 4 epochs, batch 16,
learning rate 3e-5, 256 tokens, 25.2 seconds on M4 MPS. The saved checkpoint is
55 MB. The script is `bench/finetune/train_tinybert.py`. The saved epoch is
selected on validation with a 10x penalty for a false negative, plus smaller
penalties for a wasted extraction and a modality error.

A 421M Laya fine-tune was attempted first. On this 16 GB M4 it began swapping
about 4 GB; the full-encoder run was abandoned at 6 percent, and a frozen-head
run stalled in its calibration pass. That is a deployment finding: a 421M gate
is not free on a laptop-class host. The 55 MB checkpoint is the recorded run. A
CUDA or larger-memory run may do better.

## Result

### 12-case eval

| Arm | Threshold | Commitments kept | Clean status skipped | Info-only kept | Manifest presence | Modality |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zero-shot Laya (`21-model-comparison.md`) | 0.50 | 10 of 10 (kept all 12) | no | yes | 11 of 12 agreement | 8 of 11 |
| TinyBERT gate | 0.20 | 10 of 10 | yes | yes | 12 of 12 | 8 of 10 commitments |
| TinyBERT gate | 0.50 | 10 of 10 | yes | yes | 12 of 12 | 8 of 10 commitments |

Laya's modality denominator includes the info-only case. The TinyBERT rows count
the 10 commitment cases; its all-entity accuracy is 8 of 11, the same headline
as Laya's 8 of 11.

The gate keeps every commitment and skips the clean status check. It keeps the
info-only policy case (`t07`). Under the strict commitment label that is a false
positive. Under the extractor's manifest it is a correct keep, because `t07`
holds an `info_only` entity; the recorded extraction returns an empty list and
the outcome is green either way. The gate therefore agrees with the extractor's
manifest on all 12 cases.

Per case at threshold 0.50:

```
case                      p_needs   decision pred         gold         ok
t01_phantom_992            0.9923   keep     binding      binding      yes
t02_phantom_1042           0.9953   keep     binding      binding      yes
t03_clean                  0.0131   skip     info_only    info_only    yes
t04_settled                0.9946   keep     binding      binding      yes
t05_retracted              0.9881   keep     superseded   retracted    no
t06_conditional            0.9964   keep     conditional  conditional  yes
t07_info_only              0.9962   keep     binding      info_only    no
t08_superseded             0.9934   keep     superseded   superseded   yes
t09_no_order               0.9966   keep     binding      binding      yes
t10_cancel                 0.9585   keep     retracted    binding      no
t11_multi                  0.9902   keep     binding      binding      yes
t12_decimal                0.9892   keep     binding      binding      yes
```

The three `no` rows are modality errors, not gate errors. The gate keeps all
three, so the extractor still sees them. The binary decision is clean on this
set.

### Held-out synthetic and wild text

| Threshold | Test recall | Test skip | Test modality (commitments) | Bitext discarded | Bitext mean p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.20 | 98.4% | 98.4% | 88.0% | 6.3% | 0.854 |
| 0.50 | 96.0% | 98.4% | 88.0% | 12.7% | 0.854 |
| 0.064 (safe on val) | 99.2% | 97.6% | 88.0% | 2.7% | 0.854 |

Test recall is the fraction of the 125 held-out commitments kept. Test skip is
the fraction of the 125 held-out non-commitments skipped. Test modality is the
five-way accuracy on the 125 commitments. The Bitext sample is
the same 300 money-adjacent rows used in `19-hf-benchmark.md`. It has no labels
for our schema, so it measures cost, not accuracy. Zero-shot Laya kept 98
percent of it (2 percent discard). The TinyBERT gate discards 6.3 percent at
0.20 and 12.7 percent at 0.50. The mean promise probability of 0.854 shows the
model is conservative on this sample; the discard comes from the cases it
confidently rejects.

The sample is money-adjacent by construction: 150 of 300 rows are refund
intents. A general support queue would likely discard more, but we have not
measured one. Do not quote 12.7 percent as a general rate.

### Calibration and latency

- Brier 0.016 and ECE 0.022 on the 250-row held-out set, after one temperature
  fit on 250 validation rows (`T=0.8`).
- 1.0 ms per case on the 250-row sets, 3.6 ms per case on the 300-row Bitext
  set, batch 32, M4 MPS, 55 MB checkpoint.

## Reading

The free gate is real on this set. It keeps all 10 commitments in the committed
12-case eval, skips the clean status check, and agrees with the extractor's
manifest on all 12 cases. On 250 held-out synthetic rows it keeps 98.4 percent
of commitments and skips 98.4 percent of non-commitments at threshold 0.20. It
also discards 6.3 percent of the 300 money-adjacent Bitext rows, against 2
percent for zero-shot Laya. At 0.50 the discard rises to 12.7 percent for a
2.4-point recall cost on the held-out set.

For a product that fails closed on money, 0.20 is the better default: it keeps
98.4 percent of held-out commitments and still discards 6.3 percent of the wild
sample. 0.50 is the cost-tuned point, not the safety point.

The modality head is not the reason to ship this. It gets 8 of 10 commitment
states right on the eval. It confuses a retraction with a supersession and a
cancellation with a retraction. The gate decision is unaffected: it keeps all
three, and the extractor classifies them. The gate's job is the binary call,
and that call is clean on the eval.

## Limits

- Training data is synthetic. It covers promise shapes, not real merchant
  transcripts.
- The primary eval is 12 cases. The held-out synthetic set is larger but shares
  the generator's author bias.
- One training run. No seed sweep, so variance is unmeasured.
- The 421M Laya path was not completed on this machine. This note does not claim
  that a 55 MB checkpoint beats a larger one.
- The gate is not wired into `/audit`. It is a 55 MB checkpoint plus a small
  torch runtime. Shipping it is a deployment decision, not a research result.
- The Bitext sample has no labels for our schema. The discard rate measures
  cost, not recall.
- The TinyBERT model card does not state a license. Verify it before
  redistributing the checkpoint; the method transfers to a checkpoint with a
  clear license.

## Decision

Keep the hosted extractor as the product path. The TinyBERT gate is a credible
free alternative for a batch worker, where a 55 MB checkpoint and a few
milliseconds per transcript are cheap and the cold start is amortized. It is
not a drop-in replacement for the synchronous API on this evidence, and it is
not wired in.
The next step, if the product needs the saving, is to run the gate over a real
merchant transcript sample and compare its discard rate and false-clear rate.

## Reproduce

```bash
cd bench/finetune
uv venv .venv --python 3.12
uv pip install --python .venv/bin/python torch transformers pandas pyarrow
.venv/bin/python generate_data.py --out data
.venv/bin/python train_tinybert.py \
  --data data \
  --output out/tinybert_gate \
  --epochs 4 --batch-size 16 --device mps
.venv/bin/python eval_tinybert.py \
  --model out/tinybert_gate \
  --device mps
.venv/bin/python summarize.py \
  --report out/tinybert_eval_report.json \
  --out results/summary.json
```

The recorded environment used `torch` 2.15.0 and `transformers` 5.22.1. Add
`laya==0.4.21` only for the larger 421M path in `train_gate.py`. The first run
downloads the 120 MB TinyBERT base weights from Hugging Face.

Compact evidence: `bench/finetune/results/summary.json` holds the per-case eval
probabilities and the headline metrics.
